# Strategy vector catalog

Use these vectors as prompts for causal analysis. A vector is neither inherently good nor a moat by itself. Select it only when evidence supports the mechanism in the current arena, and evaluate combinations for reinforcement and conflict.

## Accumulation

| Vector | Mechanism | Illustrative patterns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Usership | A critical mass of participants increases utility or reach. | Telephone networks, the internet, scaled social networks |
| Completeness | One system satisfies most related needs and reduces multi-tool coordination. | Swiss army knives, one-stop retailers |
| Aggregation | A shared environment concentrates diverse supply, demand, or participation. | Cities, malls, broad marketplaces, classifieds |
| Diversification | Multiple resource streams reduce dependence on any single one. | Conglomerates, omnivores |

## Price

| Vector | Mechanism | Illustrative patterns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affordability | A lower total cost expands access or wins price-sensitive demand. | Mass production, fast food, fast fashion |
| Luxury | Deliberately high cost, scarcity, labor, or signaling creates status and margin. | Supercars, luxury watches, Veblen goods |
| Skimming | A small share of a very large transaction or resource flow accumulates value. | Payment rails, baleen whales, planktivores |

## Time

| Vector | Mechanism | Illustrative patterns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heritage | A multigenerational record carries trust, identity, or accumulated legitimacy. | Religions, old institutions, legacy companies |
| Craftsmanship | Extra time, precision, and attention create a quality competitors do not economically match. | Bespoke tailoring, handmade watches, precision optics |
| Organic | A natural or path-dependent growth process cannot be compressed without losing value. | Trees, ecosystems, aged cheese |
| Endurance | Continuous operation outlasts competitors, interruptions, or resource cycles. | Marathon runners, long-range submarines |

## Uniqueness

| Vector | Mechanism | Illustrative patterns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specialization | Deep competence serves a narrow niche better than general alternatives. | Trade schools, pandas, specialist tools |
| Versatility | General competence transfers across many tasks or environments. | CPUs, opposable thumbs, multipurpose platforms |
| Hybrid | Two normally separate strengths combine into a useful whole. | Amphibians, amphibious vehicles, hybrid cars |
| Divergence | Unusual form or thought differentiates the subject from the norm. | Distinctive artists, unconventional architecture |
| Authenticity | Verifiable provenance resists counterfeiting and supports trust. | Currency, provenance-tracked art, certified foods |
| Rarity | Naturally limited supply or an extreme position in a distribution creates value. | Beachfront property, exceptional physical traits |
| Scarcity | Controlled supply limits access and can concentrate demand. | Limited editions, collectibles |
| Secrecy | Protected knowledge or entangled know-how raises replication cost. | Trade secrets, magician methods, secret formulas |
| Irreverence | Contrarian or countercultural behavior attracts people underserved by convention. | Satire, challenger brands, unconventional leaders |

## Offense

Use this category to recognize adversarial pressure and model defenses. Do not operationalize harm, intrusion, theft, deception, harassment, or unlawful exclusion.

| Vector | Diagnostic mechanism | Illustrative patterns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Violence | Physical force destroys, consumes, or excludes an opponent. | Predation, armed conflict |
| Litigation | Legal burden or exclusion weakens an opponent. | Lawsuits, aggressive patent enforcement |
| Nettlesomeness | Persistent pressure induces errors or costly reactions. | Positional pressure in chess, disruptive competitive moves |
| Sabotage | Deliberate interference creates weaknesses in an opponent's structure or defenses. | Infrastructure tampering, supply interference |
| Parasitism | A dependent actor extracts or redirects a host's resources. | Mosquitoes, parasitic fungi, resource hijacking |
| Scavenging | An actor captures resources left by weakened or failed competitors. | Vultures, distressed-asset acquisition |
| Espionage | Infiltration exposes protected information. | Spies, trade-secret theft |
| Swarming | Many individually weak agents concentrate pressure at once. | Bees, denial-of-service attacks, mass rushes |
| Lure | An attractive signal draws a target into a trap. | Anglerfish, honeypots |
| Highest bidder | Excess willingness to pay secures scarce or exclusive resources. | Elite talent acquisition, auctions, exclusive rights |

## Defense

| Vector | Mechanism | Illustrative patterns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deterrence | Credible retaliation makes attack uneconomic. | Mutual defense, enforceable trademarks and patents |
| Reliability | Low failure and maintenance burden reduce lifecycle cost and risk. | Durable ignition tools, high-reliability vehicles |
| Predictability | Repeatable delivery reduces uncertainty and builds habit or trust. | Standardized hospitality, chain retailers |
| Unpredictability | Surprise and variability make targeting or anticipation difficult. | Squid ink, smoke screens, anonymous art interventions |
| Decentralization | Distributed capability and redundancy remove single points of failure. | Distributed energy, grassroots movements |
| Security | Controls resist theft, confiscation, damage, or unauthorized access. | Vaults, locks, hardened systems |
| Privacy | Information boundaries prevent unwanted disclosure or observation. | Encrypted messaging, privacy networks |
| Durability | Physical robustness resists damage, wear, or injury. | Cast-iron cookware, resilient athletes |
| Neutrality | Durable non-belligerence makes the subject a trusted safe intermediary. | Neutral states, humanitarian intermediaries |
| Obscurity | Low visibility reduces detection, attention, or attack. | Deep-sea organisms, low-profile systems |
| Decoy | A false target redirects observation or attack. | Chaff, dummy equipment, defensive honeypots |
| Antifragility | Bounded stress, variation, or usage improves capability. | Muscles, peer-to-peer distribution under load |

## Timing

| Vector | Mechanism | Illustrative patterns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First-mover | Early action captures learning, scarce assets, standards, or participant habit. | Early streaming platforms, early category entrants |
| Second-mover | A fast follower learns from the pioneer and avoids its mistakes. | Feature followers, improved category entrants |
| Last-mover | A patient entrant waits for failed approaches and enabling conditions to become clear. | Late category-defining products, mature-market entrants |

## Accreditation

| Vector | Mechanism | Illustrative patterns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monopoly | Governing authority grants or protects exclusive control of a resource or market. | Patents, exclusive licenses, regulated concessions |
| Prestige | Recognition from authoritative institutions transfers trust or status. | Major awards, selective degrees, expert ratings |
| Curation | Superior selection and grouping reduce search cost or signal taste. | Galleries, selective media brands, investment portfolios |

## Collaboration

| Vector | Mechanism | Illustrative patterns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Union | Potential competitors adopt shared governance and reduce internal friction. | Federations, mergers, political unions |
| Alliance | Independent actors coordinate to gain benefits neither can obtain alone. | Partnerships, defense or distribution alliances |
| Emergence | Many local contributions produce higher-order intelligence or capability. | Ant colonies, beehives, crowdsourced systems |
| Centralization | A single decision center creates coherence and rapid coordination. | Command organizations, tightly controlled firms |
| Standardization | Shared rules or interfaces reduce coordination and switching friction. | Languages, railway gauges, measurement systems |
| Symbiosis | Mutually beneficial dependence makes both participants stronger together. | Fig trees and fig wasps, complementary platforms |
| Herding | Grouping protects individually weaker participants or amplifies their signal. | Trade associations, schools of fish |
| Distributed ownership | Community ownership aligns participants with the system's survival. | Cooperatives, community-owned teams |
| Transparency | Visible processes and internals lower verification cost and build trust. | Open-source software, public audit trails |

## Speed and scale

| Vector | Mechanism | Illustrative patterns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Iteration | Short feedback cycles increase the rate of useful learning and improvement. | Rapid launch-test loops, iterative engineering |
| Efficiency | Comparable capability consumes fewer resources. | LED lighting, lean processes |
| Agility | Low switching cost and adaptable structure respond quickly to change. | Gymnasts, squirrels, lightweight vehicles |
| Precision | High accuracy and low variance win tasks where error is expensive. | CNC machining, scientific instruments |
| Blitzing | Concentrated resources create a short, overwhelming burst of action. | Hostile takeovers, launch blitzes |
| Composability | Components can be assembled into new workflows and configurations. | APIs, programmable payment systems |
| Modularity | Independent units can be replaced and recombined without rebuilding the whole. | Construction blocks, prefabricated buildings |

## Ease

| Vector | Mechanism | Illustrative patterns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intuitiveness | People can understand and use the subject without explicit instruction. | Self-explanatory controls, legible industrial design |
| Fun | Enjoyment makes participation intrinsically rewarding and repeatable. | Games, amusement parks, playful learning |
| Simplicity | Few components and failure points reduce cognitive and operating burden. | Hammers, minimalist products |
| Low-friction | Minimal steps, delay, or effort improve conversion and continued use. | One-click ordering, automatic setup |
| Charm | Personality or aesthetics create affection, loyalty, and social pull. | Charismatic hosts, beloved places and brands |

## Transformation

| Vector | Mechanism | Illustrative patterns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Malleability | Form, appearance, or behavior can be customized with low effort. | Clay, software, adaptable performers |
| Metamorphosis | Distinct life-cycle states are optimized for different functions or environments. | Caterpillars and butterflies, tadpoles and frogs |
| Copycat | Proven methods or styles are replicated to reduce discovery cost. | Generic brands, fast-fashion replication |
| Camouflage | The subject blends into its environment to avoid detection or resistance. | Chameleons, stealth aircraft |
| Mimicry | Surface resemblance to another actor changes how observers respond. | Harmless species resembling dangerous ones |
